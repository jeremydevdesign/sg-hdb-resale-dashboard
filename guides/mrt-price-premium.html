Is it worth paying more to live near an MRT?

Flats near MRT stations do sell for more, but the premium is smaller than the raw numbers suggest, and in a few towns flats near the station sell for less.

SG HDB Resale· Based on 10,837 four-room resales in 2025 5 min read

A common piece of advice is to buy near an MRT station because the flat will hold its value. To test it, we took every four-room resale in 2025 and split the sales by straight-line distance to the nearest station.

On the raw numbers the advice holds up. Flats within 500 metres of a station sold for a median of $670 per square foot, against $606 for flats further out. That is a 10% premium, or about $58,000 on a typical four-room flat.

But that $58,000 gap mixes two effects, and only one of them is the MRT.

Most of the gap is location

Flats near stations are not spread evenly across Singapore. Many are in the older, central, mature estates that grew up around the rail lines. So an island-wide comparison of near and far is partly a comparison of Queenstown and Toa Payoh with Sengkang and Woodlands. A good share of what looks like an MRT premium is really a town premium.

A fairer test compares near and far flats within the same town. Across the 20 towns with enough sales on both sides, the median premium falls to about 5%, half the raw figure.

The median also hides how much the premium varies. In some towns it is very large. In others it is negative, and the flats nearest the station sell for less per square foot.

The largest premiums are in central, mature towns such as Clementi, Toa Payoh and Geylang, where a flat near the station is also close to shops, services and the city. In those towns the MRT and the wider location both push prices up.

In Tampines and Bukit Batok it works the other way. The blocks closest to the interchange are often the oldest, built when the town centre went up. Newer and larger flats came later, in precincts further from the line. In these towns a flat near the MRT is more likely to be older and smaller, and its price reflects that.

The same effect shows up in lease

Sort 2025's four-room sales by remaining lease and one band stands out:

Flats with 80–85 years left sold for a median of $845,000, more than flats with ten more years of lease. Lease alone cannot explain that. The last column shows the reason: those flats are about 350 metres from a station on average, while the bands either side of them are a kilometre or more away.

Many of the 80–85 year flats are central infill projects HDB built around 2006–2010 in Queenstown, Toa Payoh and Bukit Merah, next to existing lines. Remaining lease is closely tied to when a flat was built, and that in turn reflects where HDB was building at the time. Again, location is doing most of the work.

In both cases, being near the MRT, having more lease and selling for more tend to go together, and the common cause is mostly location. It is worth knowing this so you do not pay for the same advantage twice.

Is it worth paying more?

Usually some extra is justified. There is a real premium for being near a station, but it is smaller and more local than people tend to assume. A few things to keep in mind when an agent says a flat is worth more because of the MRT:

Practical points

  • Expect a modest premium. The island-wide gap is about 10%, and half of that is central location. Within a town, the premium for being near a station is closer to 5%.
  • Look at your own town. The premium ranges from +73% in Clementi to −16% in Bukit Batok, so the island-wide average tells you little about a particular offer.
  • Near the MRT can mean an older flat. Where the blocks next to the station are the first ones built, you may be paying extra for the shortest lease in the town.
  • Don't count the same thing twice. A central flat near the MRT with a long lease does not carry three separate premiums. They are largely the same location premium.

How we measured this

Every figure comes from the 10,837 four-room HDB resale transactions recorded in 2025 (source: data.gov.sg). "$psf" is the median of price ÷ floor area across the relevant transactions.

"Near MRT" means a straight-line distance of 500 m or less to the nearest MRT station (heavy rail; LRT not yet included) - a real walk is always a little longer. The within-town comparison includes the 20 towns with at least 30 four-room sales on each side of the 500 m line.

Medians describe past sales; they are not valuations or forecasts.
