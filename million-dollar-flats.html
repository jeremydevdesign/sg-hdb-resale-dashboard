How to read this page

The charts and tables count million-dollar sales by year and town; the records list the highest prices to date.

Million-dollar HDB flats - common questions

How many HDB flats have sold for over a million dollars?

As of September 2026, 5,622 HDB resale flats have crossed S$1,000,000 in the public record since 2012. The pace has risen sharply: 1,497 have done so far in 2026 alone, versus 1,243 over the same months of 2025. About one in 13 resales this year has been a million-dollar sale.

What is the most expensive HDB flat ever sold?

The highest resale price in the public record is $1,728,000, for a 5-room flat in Bukit Merah (96A Henderson Rd), transacted in April 2026. Records like this are usually large, high-floor units in central or mature towns, often executive flats or maisonettes.

Which towns have the most million-dollar flats?

Central and mature towns dominate - places like Bukit Merah, Queenstown, Toa Payoh, Kallang/Whampoa and Bishan, where location, larger older units and short distances to the city push prices up. The table above ranks every town by its all-time count. Newer towns rarely appear.

Are million-dollar HDB flats overpriced?

Not necessarily - a high price usually reflects size, a high floor, a central location or a rare flat type, not a bad deal. To judge any single flat, compare it against the median for that town and flat type rather than the headline. Browse prices by town or read the guide on judging a fair price.

Where does this data come from?

Every figure is counted directly from HDB resale transactions published on data.gov.sg - actual recorded sales, not estimates or listings. The page updates as new monthly data is released. The current year is shown as year-to-date.

How we measured this

Counts are every HDB resale transaction with a recorded price of S$1,000,000 or more, taken directly from the resale dataset on data.gov.sg (registrations from 2012 onward). These are actual recorded sales, not listings, valuations or estimates. $psf converts the recorded floor area (sqm) to square feet. The current year (2026) is year-to-date and is labelled as such; it is not annualised.
